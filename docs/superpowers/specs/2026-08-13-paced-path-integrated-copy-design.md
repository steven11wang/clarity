## Objective

Create one finished landscape image that keeps the supplied Chinese ink-wash artwork intact while moving the feature title and explanation into the artwork itself. The result must feel like one editorial composition, not an illustration beside a text panel.

## Source Roles

- `/Users/s/Downloads/F1.png` is the edit target and supplies the full canvas, mountains, pavilions, bridge, mist, blue route, nodes, ornaments, palette, and texture.
- `/Users/s/Desktop/Screenshot 2026-08-13 at 19.55.15.png` is a copy and hierarchy reference only. Its split-column page layout must not be reproduced.

## Layout and Copy

- Place the eyebrow `01 · PACED PATH` in the upper-left negative space, with a short cobalt rule.
- Place the two-line headline `LEARN AT THE` / `RIGHT PACE` directly below it.
- Place the supporting copy near the lower-left edge in two restrained lines: `Follow a guided path through domains and skills. Build critical` / `thinking while vocabulary grows with every lesson.`

## Art Direction

- Headline: elegant high-contrast editorial serif, dark blue-black ink, large but not oversized.
- Eyebrow and fine rule: restrained teal/cobalt accents that echo the route line.
- Supporting copy: small, legible sans serif in muted blue-black.
- Preserve visual breathing room around the route and keep all text away from nodes, pavilions, the bridge, and dense foliage.
- The typography may pick up very subtle paper grain so it belongs to the image, but letterforms must remain crisp.

## Invariants

- Keep the original 16:10 landscape framing and artwork unchanged outside the added typography.
- Preserve every mountain, tree, pavilion, bridge, route curve, node, orange endpoint, ornamental line, and existing color relationship.
- Do not crop, repaint, extend, simplify, recolor, or rearrange the scene.
- Render the supplied English copy verbatim, exactly once, with no additional words, logos, labels, or watermark.

## Deliverable and Acceptance Criteria

Produce one high-quality PNG saved non-destructively in the workspace. It succeeds when the image still reads first as the original ink landscape, the copy is immediately readable, and the title and description feel embedded in the scene rather than placed beside it.
